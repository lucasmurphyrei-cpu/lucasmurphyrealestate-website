## County Overview

Milwaukee County is the most densely populated county in Wisconsin and the cultural, economic, and entertainment hub of the southeastern part of the state. Situated on the western shore of Lake Michigan, the county encompasses the City of Milwaukee and 18 surrounding suburbs, offering a diverse range of lifestyles from dense urban living to quiet, wooded estates.

### Major Employers

Milwaukee County is home to a powerful concentration of Fortune 500 companies and major employers across healthcare, finance, manufacturing, and technology [3]:

- **Northwestern Mutual** -- Financial services headquarters, 7,000+ local employees
- **Advocate Aurora Health** -- One of the nation's 10 largest nonprofit health systems, 70,000+ employees regionwide
- **Johnson Controls** -- Global building technology and solutions
- **Rockwell Automation** -- Industrial automation headquarters
- **Kohl's Corporation** -- Retail headquarters (Menomonee Falls)
- **Fiserv** -- Financial technology (Brookfield)
- **GE Healthcare** -- Medical technology
- **ManpowerGroup** -- Global staffing and workforce solutions
- **WEC Energy Group** -- Regional utility
- **Harley-Davidson** -- Iconic motorcycle manufacturer
- **Milwaukee Tool** -- Power tools and equipment
- **Froedtert Health** -- Academic medical center

### Transportation & Infrastructure

Milwaukee County is served by the Milwaukee County Transit System (MCTS), Milwaukee Mitchell International Airport (MKE), Amtrak Hiawatha service to Chicago, and a growing network of bike lanes and the 125-mile Oak Leaf Trail system. Major freeways include I-94, I-43, and I-894/US-45 [4].

### Buyer & Lifestyle Fit

**Ideal Buyer:** Young professionals, first-time homebuyers, artists, investors, and urban enthusiasts who want affordability, culture, and walkability in a major metropolitan setting.

**Commute to Downtown:** 0-20 minutes depending on neighborhood (many neighborhoods are within downtown itself) [4].

**Walkability & Transportation:** Walk Score of 62 ("Somewhat Walkable") [9]. The most walkable neighborhoods -- Juneau Town (Walk Score 93), Lower East Side (Walk Score 89), and Yankee Hill (Walk Score 91) -- rival Chicago's best [9]. MCTS bus service covers the city extensively, and the Oak Leaf Trail provides over 125 miles of dedicated bike paths. Quick freeway access via I-94, I-43, and I-794 [4].

**Crime/Safety:** CrimeGrade overall D+ for Milwaukee County. Safety varies dramatically by neighborhood -- the southwest and far northwest sides are considerably safer than the north-central core. Neighborhoods like Bay View, the East Side, and the Third Ward have notably lower crime rates [2].

### Amenities & Character

**Major Parks, Restaurants & Attractions:**
- **Fiserv Forum & Deer District** -- Home of the NBA's Milwaukee Bucks, major concerts, and the vibrant Deer District entertainment plaza [10]
- **Milwaukee Art Museum** -- Iconic Santiago Calatrava-designed "wings" on the lakefront [10]
- **Historic Third Ward** -- Converted warehouses now home to boutiques like Milwaukee Public Market, Harbor House restaurant, and Cafe Benelux [10]
- **Brady Street** -- Bohemian strip with restaurants like Balzac Wine Bar and Glorioso's Italian Market
- **Lakefront Brewery** -- Award-winning craft brewery with popular Friday fish fry tours
- **Pabst Mansion** -- Gilded Age mansion museum
- **Harley-Davidson Museum** -- Celebrating the iconic motorcycle brand's 120+ year heritage

**Community Vibe:** Milwaukee is a blue-collar city with a creative soul. The city punches well above its weight in culinary innovation, craft brewing, and arts, while maintaining a Midwestern friendliness and affordability that larger lakefront cities have long lost. Neighborhoods are tightly defined and fiercely proud of their distinct identities.

**Notable Annual Events:**
- **Summerfest** (June-July) -- "The World's Largest Music Festival," 800+ acts across 12 stages at Henry Maier Festival Park [11]
- **German Fest** (July) -- Largest German cultural festival in the U.S. [11]
- **Bastille Days** (July) -- French-themed street festival at Cathedral Square Park [11]
- **Polish Fest, Irish Fest, Mexican Fiesta, Indian Summer** -- Summer-long ethnic festivals at the lakefront [11]

**Proximity to Lake Michigan:** Direct lakefront access spanning approximately 8 miles of city shoreline. Major employers Northwestern Mutual, Advocate Aurora Health, and Rockwell Automation are headquartered downtown [3].

- **Milwaukee County Zoo** -- One of the top zoos in the Midwest, located in Wauwatosa
- **Wisconsin State Fair Park** -- Adjacent to Wauwatosa (in West Allis) with year-round events

**Community Vibe:** Wauwatosa -- "Tosa" to locals -- is the gold standard of Milwaukee-area suburbs. Tree-lined streets, exceptional schools, and a walkable village center give it a small-town feel, while proximity to major retail, healthcare (Medical College of Wisconsin and Froedtert Hospital are here), and downtown Milwaukee ensure residents never feel isolated.

**Notable Annual Events:**
- **Tosa Fest** (June) -- Street festival with live music, food, and family activities
- **Wauwatosa Farmers Market** (May-October) -- Saturday mornings in Hart Park
- **Fourth of July Celebration** -- Parade and fireworks along the village

**Proximity:** Adjacent to the Medical College of Wisconsin campus and Froedtert Hospital, Milwaukee County Zoo, and major retail corridors. 10-minute drive to Lake Michigan [4].

Residents tend to be well-educated, civically engaged, and passionate about preserving the village's walkable, mixed-use character.

**Notable Annual Events:**

- **Shorewood Criterium** -- Competitive bicycle race through the village streets
- **Fourth of July Parade** -- A beloved small-town tradition

**Proximity to Lake Michigan:** Direct lakefront access at Atwater Park. Walking distance to UWM campus. 10 minutes to downtown Milwaukee [4].

### Buyer & Lifestyle Fit

**Ideal Buyer:** Young families seeking a safe, nature-rich suburb with good schools at prices below Waukesha County alternatives. First-time buyers priced out of Brookfield or New Berlin who want a suburban feel with Whitnall Park as their backyard. Nature lovers drawn to Boerner Botanical Gardens, Wehr Nature Center, and the Oak Leaf Trail. Empty nesters and retirees wanting an established neighborhood with community events and low crime.

**Commute to Downtown:** Approximately 15-20 minutes via I-43 North (~14 miles northeast of downtown Milwaukee). The Route 44U UBUS provides express commuter service from the Hales Corners Park & Ride at Highway 100 and Layton Ave.

**Walkability & Transportation:** Walk Score approximately 30 (car-dependent). The S. 108th Street commercial corridor provides some walkable amenities. MCTS bus service via Route 44U (UBUS express to downtown) and local Routes 28 and 54. The Oak Leaf Trail runs through Whitnall Park connecting to the Root River Parkway and New Berlin Recreation Trail for cycling.

**Crime/Safety:** Strong safety reputation. Crime index 5x lower than the U.S. average. Violent crime rate is 10.6 vs. the national average of 22.7. Zero reported homicides in the most recent reporting year. Residents describe the village as safe, family-friendly, and well-patrolled.

### Amenities & Character

**Major Parks, Restaurants & Attractions:**
- **Whitnall Park** -- The largest park in the Milwaukee County Parks system; trails, picnic areas, and extensive green space
- **Boerner Botanical Gardens** -- Oldest nationally recognized public garden in the Great Lakes region (designed 1930s); 12 formal gardens; 2-acre Margie's Garden for children; host of the China Lights Festival
- **Wehr Nature Center** -- 220-acre nature preserve with 5 miles of trails through woodland, wetland, prairie, oak savanna, and around Mallard Lake
- **Whitnall Park Golf Course** -- Historic 18-hole, par-71 course (opened 1932); 6,373 yards with wooded fairways
- **Clifford's Supper Club** -- Institution since 1959; classic Wisconsin supper club known for Friday Fish Fry
- **Ann's Italian Restaurant** -- Long-standing local favorite with extensive Italian menu

**Community Vibe:** A tight-knit village of 7,600 where neighbors recognize each other and local merchants actively support community programs. Hales Corners offers a dense suburban feel with a genuine small-town heart -- families gather for Fourth of July parades, golfers play the historic Whitnall Park course, and nature lovers walk the trails at Wehr Nature Center year-round. Few suburbs of this size can claim a 220-acre nature preserve, a world-class botanical garden, and a historic 18-hole golf course all within their borders.

**Notable Annual Events:**
- **Fourth of July Celebration** -- Full-day event: Firecracker Four 4-mile run, Children's Parade (9:30 AM), The Big Parade (4:00 PM), bed races, hot dog eating contest, live entertainment, and fireworks at dusk
- **China Lights Festival** (September-November) -- 40+ larger-than-life Chinese lantern displays at Boerner Botanical Gardens; one of Milwaukee's most popular fall attractions
- **Holz Motors Car Show** (July 4) -- Benefits the Hales Corners Lions Club

**Proximity to Lake Michigan / Major Employers:** Not lakefront. Approximately 15 miles inland from Lake Michigan. South Shore Park and Bay View lakefront are 20-25 minutes east via I-894/I-94.. Holz Motors (family-owned Chevy dealership since 1914, Wisconsin's #1 Chevrolet dealer for 30 consecutive years), Whitnall School District, Milwaukee County Parks operations. Proximity to employment in downtown Milwaukee (14 mi), Brookfield/New Berlin (10 mi west), and Franklin/Oak Creek (5-10 mi).
